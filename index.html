La Ficción como Lugar

Instalación en técnica mixta · 2026 · Obra única

“La ficción como lugar” is not an installation about displacement. It is an installation that practises displacement: converting it into a working method, a system of translation between materials, a generative grammar of possibilities. Being foreign is not the subject; it is the condition from which everything else is produced.

The conceptual framework is speculative fiction understood not as a literary genre but as a method. Each piece of the installation is an act of speculation: it proposes an alternative version of something known, a “what if?” materialised into an object.

The installation does not illustrate a thesis about exile: it practises it. Each piece is an act of translation, and translation always transforms what it touches. Making with the hands is also a form of knowing: a knowledge that emerges from the process itself, from the resistance of the material, from error, from the decision taken at the moment when the hands are already working.

Archivo Múltiple

Mixed Media Installation · 2025 · Unique work

A speculative archive that does not document the past, but generates possible fictions from everyday objects. Modular ceramic patterns, embroidery, drawings and daily-use materials function as a classification system of the imaginary: not a record of what was, but an open proposal of what could be.

The project investigates how everyday objects carry latent memories that different audiences can activate, reinterpret or contradict. Through a participatory website, the viewer can contribute their own fictions, turning the archive into a collective organism in constant expansion.

The installation proposes speculative fiction not as evasion, but as a method of knowledge, learning and adaptation: a tool for inhabiting the space between what existed, what is remembered, and what is invented from within each person.

Glazed ceramic, embroidery, UV resin, wire, thread, acetate, watercolour

Un corazón abierto

Mixed Media Installation · 2025 · Unique work

Open Heart explores the boundary between the living and the inanimate, inviting reflection on the human need to give soul and presence to everyday objects. Trees, stones, fabrics — objects we consider inert — become possible interlocutors.

The work does not impose meaning, but proposes a sensitive experiment. Through natural materials and mythopoetic references, it invites the viewer to approach, observe, touch and feel. Each person activates the installation from their own individual and cultural memory.

This multiplicity of interpretations is central to the project: the installation functions as a threshold space where collective fiction and individual perception contaminate each other.

Wood, fabric, paper, cardboard, natural elements, acetate

Archivo Familiar

Photography & Collage · A5 · 2025 · Unique work

Archivo Familiar is a project based on a reflection on the humanisation of the inanimate — giving feelings and character to toys, trees, stones, water, clouds, as if trying to listen to their inner voice. This is not only a way of simplifying perception, but also a profoundly human need: not to be alone.

This approach has its roots in animism and esoteric practices. In modern times, when more and more of life is spent online — where everything is recorded but little is truly felt — there is an acute need to reconnect with the real and tangible world.

Techno Witch

Immersive Installation · 2024 · Salamanca · Unique work

Techno Witch is an immersive installation exploring the frontier between ancestral spirituality and contemporary digital culture. The work recreates the room of a modern witch inviting the public to participate in a real-time oracular reading.

The project starts from a question about collective memory: why, in a world saturated with information, do people still turn to rituals, symbols and archetypes to understand uncertainty?

The public does not observe from outside — they enter, touch the objects and participate in the ritual. Each visitor activates the space from their own cultural memory: what for one is play, for another is belief; what for one is fiction, for another is real.

Paper, wood, fabrics, UV resin, ritual objects
200 × 200 cm

Artist Statement

“My practice sits at the threshold between collective memory and shared fiction: that space where what we remember becomes something we inhabit as if it were real.”

Anastasiia Lantukh (Glukhiv, Sumy, Ukraine, 2003) is a multidisciplinary artist currently living and working in Spain. Her life path, marked by displacement between countries due to the war, has shaped an open, sensitive gaze in constant search of connection with other cultures and ways of thinking.

She works primarily with sculpture and installation, exploring materials such as wood, fabric, thread, paper, clay, metal, resin and ceramics. She combines mixed techniques and manual processes, giving prominence to matter and feeling.

Her practice revolves around the boundary between reality and fiction. Inspired by magic, traditions and speculative imagination, she uses creation as a means to reflect on the invisible, the symbolic and what connects human beings beyond language.
